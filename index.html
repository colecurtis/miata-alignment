This is a DIY measurement aid, not a calibrated alignment rack. Phone tilt sensors can help compare camber; toe depends on careful string and tape setup, and caster depends on accurate front-wheel turn angles. Verify final settings with a professional alignment system before spirited or road use.

How to measure, and what the math means

1. Prepare the car

Use a level surface, set tire pressures consistently, settle the suspension, and keep the car at a repeatable load and ride height. Use a rigid phone adapter that sits flat on the rim, always on the same contact surfaces. The phone is a measuring instrument here; it does not tell you which adjuster or shim to turn.

2. Camber from gravity

With the screen facing outward and the phone held in the wheel plane, enable the sensor, select a corner, wait for a stable reading, and record it. The app estimates gravity by subtracting linear acceleration from acceleration including gravity, then averages a short window:

g = accelerationIncludingGravity - acceleration
camber ≈ -mean(atan2(gz, sqrt(gx^2 + gy^2)))

The minus sign matches the app's positive-camber convention. The APK takes two readings in opposite phone orientations and sets offset = (position1 - position2) / 2. The website now has the same two-position offset control. Calibrate on a verified zero-camber plane, then the website computes -mean(rawAngle - offset); an offset over 1.25 degrees is flagged for recheck.

3. Sensor toe from yaw

Enable sensors from the Camber screen, then open Measure > Toe > 6-point sensor. Keep the adapter's Front mark aimed toward the car's front and place it identically for each capture. Hold still until ready and capture in this order:

  1. Left rear
  2. Right rear
  3. Left rear again
  4. Left front
  5. Right front
  6. Left front again

The phone supplies a yaw-like heading. The shortest signed difference wraps across north, so 359.9° and 0.1° are only 0.2° apart:

wrap180(x) = ((x + 180) mod 360 + 360) mod 360 - 180
pairToe = -wrap180(leftHeading - rightHeading)

The app averages the first and last paired comparisons when one side has an extra reading. To estimate individual front toe, it compares a front wheel's heading with the same-side rear reference, corrects by half the rear total, and splits the front total around that reference. The two side-derived estimates are circularly averaged. This reproduces the APK's relative-to-rear calculation; rear toe split and magnetic disturbance remain sources of error.

relative = wrap180(rearYaw - frontYaw)
sideBase = sideSign * rearTotal / 2 + relative
sideOffset = wrap180(sideBase - sideSign * frontTotal / 2)
leftToe = frontTotal / 2 + sideOffset
rightToe = frontTotal / 2 - sideOffset

4. String or laser reference toe

Set parallel reference lines from the car's centerline. Compensate for different front/rear track widths; do not assume the body panels are centered over the hubs. Use a four-way laser to establish or check a vertical reference plane only if you can align it to the car centerline and verify it at both axles. Transfer the floor line to rim height with a square or plumb target. For each wheel, measure the gap at matching front and rear rim points and enter their actual span:

wheelToe = atan((frontGap - rearGap) / span)
axleToe = leftWheelToe + rightWheelToe

5. Caster estimate

Use turn plates or a wheel-angle gauge to steer a front wheel by equal angles left and right while keeping the phone fixed. Capture camber at both positions. The app estimates caster using the camber sweep:

caster = abs(asin((tan(camLeft) - tan(camRight)) / (2 sin(wheelTurnAngle))))

The APK's guide describes a straight-ahead position plus opposite turns, but its exact caster calculation was not recovered. This app's two-turn estimate is a separate approximation.

6. Use a laser or photo carefully

Your four-way laser can establish or verify a straight reference plane. First align that plane with a chassis or hub-center datum and check it at both axles; the self-leveling feature does not square it to the car. Compensate for different front/rear track widths. Transfer a floor line up to rim height with a square or plumb target before measuring the gaps.

A plain phone photo is only a visual cross-check. Perspective and lens distortion shift apparent angles. A future photo tool would need a rigid target with known dimensions, a plumb/laser reference in the wheel plane, a camera square to that plane, and lens calibration.

How the APK informed this app

The supplied APK's bundled JavaScript revealed its gravity sensor path, two-position calibration offset, circular yaw comparison, individual-front calculation, six-capture order, and adapter requirements. Those toe conventions and calibration math are now in the website. The website also keeps a line-and-tape fallback and a separate approximate caster sweep.
